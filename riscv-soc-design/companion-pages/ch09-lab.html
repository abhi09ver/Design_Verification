Experiment 1 · Sections 9.2.2 and 9.5.3

Address now, data next: who owns the Data phase decides HREADY

Model rules. Each transfer spends one or more cycles in its Address phase, accepted on an edge where HREADY = 1, then a Data phase that lasts until its subordinate raises HREADYOUT. HSEL decodes the live address; HSELD is HSEL captured when HREADY is high, and it selects the HREADY/HRDATA mux. The single-port SRAM adds one wait state when a write is directly followed by a read.

A beat moves only on VALID and READY together

Click the cells to draw one AXI channel. A transfer happens on every cycle where VALID = 1 and READY = 1. The source must keep VALID high and the payload stable until that happens. The destination may raise READY before or after VALID. Click a DATA cell to change the payload.

Experiment 3 · Section 9.5.2

The LSU wins; the IFU's address is saved and replayed

The IFU fetches every cycle. Tick the cycles where the LSU also requests (zero-wait devices). On a collision the EBU grants the LSU. The IFU saw HREADY = 1 and moved on, so the EBU saves its Address-phase signals and replays them next cycle while holding IFUHREADY low. This is a simplified version of book Figs. 9.26 and 9.27.

Experiment 4 · Sections 9.5.1 and Lab 6

What a bus costs in CPI

Wally's busfsm stalls the whole pipeline for Adr + Data: 2 cycles plus wait states per access, even with a zero-wait RAM. Lab 6 measured exactly that (128 accesses, 256 stall cycles). An APB access adds the bridge's Setup and Access cycles.
