2The five rules

#RuleWhy
R1Source must not wait for READY before asserting VALID.If both sides wait for each other → deadlock.
R2Once VALID is HIGH it stays HIGH until the handshake.Destination may sample any later cycle; data can't vanish.
R3While VALID is HIGH and not yet accepted, payload stays stable.Destination must get exactly what was offered.
R4Destination may wait for VALID before asserting READY.Lets slaves be simple and save power.
R5READY may be asserted early and may be de-asserted before VALID arrives.READY is only a "can accept now" hint until VALID shows up.
Asymmetry to remember: VALID is a commitment (can't take it back). READY is an offer (can withdraw it while VALID is low).

4Cross-channel dependencies

Read
ARVALID+ARREADYmust both happen beforeRVALID
ARREADY itself may be asserted before or after ARVALID.
Write (AXI4)
AW handshake+W handshake with WLASTmust both happen beforeBVALID
  • Slave may wait for AWVALID, WVALID or both before raising AWREADY.
  • Slave may wait for AWVALID, WVALID or both before raising WREADY.
  • Master must not wait for AWREADY before WVALID, nor for WREADY before AWVALID.
  • AXI3 was looser: BVALID only had to follow WLAST, not AW.
Mental model: a slave can always say "I'll wait for you". A master can never say "you go first".

5Timing & implementation notes

  • Everything is sampled on the rising edge of ACLK.
  • No combinational path from inputs to outputs on an interface — e.g. READY must not be a pure function of VALID through logic in the same cycle at the interface boundary.
  • READY travels backward; long READY paths hurt timing → insert a register slice.
  • A full-throughput register slice needs a 2-entry skid buffer: when READY is registered, one extra beat may arrive after downstream stalls, so it must be stored.
  • During reset, all VALIDs LOW; first VALID only after ARESETn HIGH.
  • Good practice: masters keep BREADY / RREADY HIGH unless they truly can't accept.
Throughput math: efficiency = handshakes ÷ cycles. 4 beats over 6 cycles = 67%; 2 bubbles came from back-pressure or idle VALID.

6Handshake assertions (SVA)

R2 — VALID held until READY
property p_valid_hold;
  @(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
  AWVALID && !AWREADY |=> AWVALID;
endproperty
R3 — payload stable while stalled
property p_payload_stable;
  @(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
  AWVALID && !AWREADY |=>
    $stable({AWADDR, AWLEN, AWSIZE, AWBURST, AWID});
endproperty
Reset & X checks
a_rst_low : assert property (@(posedge ACLK) !ARESETn |-> !AWVALID);
a_no_x    : assert property (@(posedge ACLK) disable iff (!ARESETn)
                             !$isunknown(AWVALID));
Copy the same set for W, AR (master side) and B, R (slave side). Add a liveness/timeout check: VALID must eventually see READY within N cycles.

?Handshake interview Q&A

QuestionCrisp answer
When exactly does a transfer happen?Rising edge where VALID and READY are both HIGH.
Can VALID depend on READY?No — that's the deadlock rule (R1).
Can READY depend on VALID?Yes — destination may wait for VALID.
Can READY drop without a transfer?Yes, if VALID was LOW.
Can VALID drop without a transfer?No — protocol violation.
Max throughput of one channel?One transfer per clock (VALID & READY both held HIGH).
Why a skid buffer in a register slice?Registered READY lags by a cycle; the in-flight beat needs a slot.
How do you test the handshake in a TB?Randomize VALID/READY delays incl. 0; cover cases A/B/C; SVA for hold/stable/reset.
Bug: slave waits for WVALID before AWREADY, master waits for AWREADY before WVALID?Deadlock; the master is the one breaking the rule.
